Learn the 5 attitudes

What is a hazardous attitude?

An attitude is the way you think about something. Most crashes aren’t caused by a broken airplane. They happen because a pilot made a bad choice, and bad choices usually start with a risky way of thinking.

The FAA (the government group in charge of flying in the U.S.) lists five hazardous attitudes. Each one has an antidote. Just like medicine for poison, an antidote is a thought a pilot can say to themselves to fix the risky thinking.

How to play

  1. Read what the pilot is thinking, saying, or doing.
  2. Look for clues. Is the pilot breaking a rule? Rushing? Showing off?
  3. Tap the attitude (or antidote) that fits best.
  4. Read the explanation so you know why. That’s how you get better!

    Watch out for look-alikes 👀

    Macho vs. Invulnerability: A macho pilot knows there’s danger but thinks “I’m good enough to handle it.” An invulnerable pilot thinks “There’s no danger for me at all.”

    Anti-authority vs. Impulsivity: An anti-authority pilot breaks a rule on purpose because they don’t like being told what to do. An impulsive pilot moves too fast to think about the rule.

    Resignation vs. Anti-authority: A resigned pilot just goes along with whatever happens or whatever others say. An anti-authority pilot pushes back against others.
